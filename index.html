About My Research

General Relativity (GR) remains our most successful theory of gravitation, having passed a wide range of experimental and observational tests over more than a century. Nevertheless, several astrophysical and cosmological observations motivate investigations into extensions of GR, including theories of modified gravity. Among these, f(R) gravity provides a particularly interesting framework in which the gravitational action is generalized by replacing the Ricci scalar R with a function f(R). The resulting higher-order field equations offer a rich setting for exploring gravitational phenomena beyond Einstein's theory, although obtaining non-trivial exact solutions remains a significant challenge.

A central question in black-hole physics concerns the uniqueness of black-hole solutions and the validity of the no-hair paradigm beyond GR. In Einstein's theory, under appropriate assumptions, stationary, asymptotically flat black holes are characterized by a small set of conserved parameters, such as mass, electric charge, and angular momentum. Whether this uniqueness extends to modified theories of gravity remains an important open question. In particular, it is essential to determine whether higher-order curvature terms permit black-hole geometries that differ fundamentally from the Kerr family and, if so, how such solutions can be distinguished through their physical properties and astrophysical signatures.

My doctoral research at the Indian Institute of Technology (IIT) Bombay, under the supervision of Professor S. Shankaranarayanan , focused on black-hole solutions in f(R) gravity and the implications of higher-order curvature corrections for black-hole uniqueness. I investigated the structure of static, spherically symmetric vacuum solutions and demonstrated the existence of infinitely degenerate solutions within the class of models considered. I also obtained two distinct exact black-hole solutions that share the same event horizon, illustrating how the solution space can differ from that of General Relativity and establishing a violation of the conventional Birkhoff uniqueness property in the models studied.

Building on these results, I constructed multiple slowly rotating black-hole solutions in vacuum for a class of f(R) gravity theories. This work revealed how the generalized Bianchi identity constrains the Ricci scalar and permits a richer family of stationary geometries than is available in standard General Relativity. Together, these investigations demonstrate that black-hole uniqueness and the associated no-hair properties depend on the underlying gravitational theory and need not persist in higher-order curvature models.

My broader research interests lie in black-hole physics, gravitational perturbation theory, and the phenomenology of gravity beyond Einstein's theory. I am particularly interested in understanding how the structure of black holes, their stability, and their characteristic oscillation modes can provide theoretical and observational tests of modified gravity.
