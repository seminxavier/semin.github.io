About Me

My research interests lie in black-hole physics, gravitational perturbation theory, and the phenomenology of gravity beyond Einstein's theory. I am particularly interested in understanding how black-hole structure, stability, and characteristic oscillation modes can provide theoretical and observational tests of modified gravity.

My doctoral research at the Indian Institute of Technology (IIT) Bombay, under the supervision of Professor S. Shankaranarayanan , focused on black-hole solutions in f(R) gravity and the implications of higher-order curvature corrections for black-hole uniqueness. I investigated static, spherically symmetric vacuum solutions and constructed multiple slowly rotating black-hole solutions for a class of f(R) gravity models, demonstrating how the solution space can extend beyond that of General Relativity.

A central question in my research is whether the uniqueness of black-hole solutions and the no-hair paradigm extend beyond General Relativity. In particular, I am interested in whether higher-order curvature terms permit black-hole geometries that differ from the Kerr family and how their physical properties and astrophysical signatures can help distinguish modified gravity from Einstein's theory.
